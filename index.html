Multi-domain scientific stack audit

Sustained reasoning under live architectural change

The task involved a substantial, multi-document theoretical and quantum-physics research stack spanning information theory, observer-dependent models, mathematical formalisation, experimental design and falsifiability. The material had to be audited and revised as one interdependent object rather than as isolated documents.

This required long-horizon state retention across a multi-stage, tool-using workflow; dependency-aware change propagation; recursive consistency checking; and architecture-level verification after modification. Unlike a static benchmark, the system had to preserve global coherence while the object itself was changing, identify second-order faults created by local edits, recover from intermediate inconsistencies, and finish with a verified end state rather than a merely plausible answer.

The evaluation burden also included matched-control and ablation-style comparison. The same knowledge object was assessed in structured, flattened, module-isolated and dependency-scrambled forms, while superficial content and link volume were retained as far as possible. A controlled deformation assay then removed, reversed or substituted selected dependencies to test correction propagation, contradiction detection, cross-system reasoning and whether apparently functional output had detached from load-bearing constraints.

The difficulty was not simply context length, output volume or file count. It was maintaining constraint integrity across an evolving system where each correction could alter the meaning, validity or behaviour of distant components.

Recorded scale: 11 sequential commits across 11 interdependent files, 1,241 changed lines, complete readback and final system-level audit.

One observed live result. It is not presented as an externally replicated benchmark.
